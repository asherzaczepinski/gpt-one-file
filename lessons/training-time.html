Training Time

How long would it take to train this model properly, on your MacBook versus a RunPod pod running plain numpy? The Mac numbers are measured on your M4. The pod and GPU numbers are estimates, and they're labelled that way.

First: what's actually holding the model back

Your model already trains to its best in under 6 minutes (test loss 3.48). Training it longer makes it worse, because it starts memorizing the stories (run 5 went from 3.58 back up to 4.26). So more time alone won't make it good. To get truly good, it needs more text (and eventually a bigger model). Time is really a question of how much text you feed it.

"Tokens/sec" here means tokens trained on per second: forward + backward. Training time = how many tokens you train on ÷ tokens/sec.

Plain numpy on RunPod: the GPU does nothing

This is the important part. numpy only ever runs on the CPU. If you rent a GPU pod and run main_functionized.py or main_functionized2.py as they are, the code runs on the pod's CPU and the GPU sits there unused, while you pay for it by the hour.

And the pod's CPU won't beat your Mac: pod CPUs are server chips that are usually slower per core than an M4, and your Mac test just showed this code doesn't get faster with more cores. So expect about the same speed as your Mac, or slower (roughly 2,000–7,000 tokens/sec).

The only way a pod gets faster is using the GPU, which means CuPy (main_functionized3.py). That column is in the table below too, for comparison, but it's a rough estimate, since it hasn't been run on a GPU yet.

How these were worked out: A is measured (6 minutes with your one-window loop; batched it would be about 3). B = 49 million tokens ÷ 6,800 (or 3,700) tokens/sec. The plain-numpy pod column = the Mac's time scaled by the pod CPU being 2,000–7,000 tokens/sec instead of 6,800. C: the bigger model does roughly 15–20× more math per token than yours, so about 400 tokens/sec on your Mac, and 600 million ÷ 400 ≈ 17 days. The GPU column assumes a mid-range GPU is roughly 30–100× faster than one Mac core at this size, which is a guess until it's actually measured on a pod.

Bottom line:

  • A is done. Your Mac does it in 6 minutes.
  • B is very doable on your Mac: a couple of hours, plugged in with the lid open. What you'd need is more story text, not more computer.
  • C is where a GPU really matters. But plain numpy on RunPod doesn't use the GPU, so it would be just as slow as your Mac. For C, run main_functionized3.py with CuPy on the pod.

Where more text would come from

The book your gpt.txt follows trains on TinyStories, a free public dataset of about 2 million short, simple children's stories (written by GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 to teach small models). It's the same kind of text as stories.txt, just hundreds of times more of it. Swapping in a bigger chunk of it is the single biggest upgrade you can make.
